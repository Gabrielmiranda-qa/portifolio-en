01 / The Problem

Nested menus cause rider distraction

6 bottom nav tabs forced eyes off the road at high speed. Unsafe screen-checking in live traffic conditions.

02 / The Decision

Single dynamic swipe action card

Replaced all tabs with one context-aware swipe card, activated automatically by the device speed sensor. Zero taps at speed.

03 / Design Tradeoff

Radically reduced information density

Show only 3 data points (speed, range, ETA) instead of the 9-metric dashboard that competitors use.

04 / Visual System

Emerald telemetry on obsidian void

Single accent colour (#10B981) for live data on deep dark background — readable in direct sunlight, no eye strain at night.

Veloce came from a personal frustration: existing commuter apps require constant interaction while riding — unlocking the phone, scrolling through tabs, reading dense data. The brief was simple: design for a rider moving at 28 km/h with one hand on the handlebar.

The key design decision was the speed-triggered context switch: below 5 km/h, the full interface is available. Above 5 km/h, the system automatically collapses everything into the single swipe action card — making safety a system behaviour, not a user choice.

01 / The Problem

Municipal forums do not reach citizens

Official channels require 10+ min forms. Participation drops to under 0.4% of affected residents.

02 / The Decision

60-second voice, zero friction

Record, tag location, publish in under 90 seconds total — no accounts, no forms. 60 seconds forces clarity of thought.

03 / Design Tradeoff

Audio-first, no text required

Chose not to add transcription or written comments — keeping voice as the primary artefact preserves emotional tone.

04 / Carioca Context

Designed for low-bandwidth Rio

Audio files cap at 120KB per recording. Works on 2G/edge networks — designed for real Rio infrastructure, not ideal conditions.

Resenha emerged from the gap between official municipal communication and street-level reality in Rio de Janeiro. Residents who experience broken sidewalks, flooded streets, and unsafe bus stops have no low-friction path to be heard.

The 60-second limit is not a technical constraint — it is a deliberate editorial decision. Long-form complaints get ignored; a focused, located 60-second voice note gets attention. The feed surfaces the most-replayed notes to neighbourhood maps, creating a ground-truth layer of civic signal that city planners can act on.

Arco is a self-initiated concept for young Brazilians opening a first account. The behaviour and metrics below are validation hypotheses.

First-account choices mix unfamiliar language, cost anxiety and too many actions at once.
